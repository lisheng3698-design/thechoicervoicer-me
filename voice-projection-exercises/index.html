The Choicer Voicer/Voice projection exercises

10 minutes · Room-distance speech · No shouting

Voice projection exercises for clear, easy reach.

Projection is not a volume contest. Train a clear line of sound with balanced posture, moving breath, bright consonants, and a specific destination—then confirm that the same phrase remains comfortable when the listener moves farther away.

Start the six-step routineTest a short scene

Steps 1–3

Build carrying power from clarity first.

Use one short phrase at an easy pitch. A partner can listen, but a marked point across the room works too.

1

Align toward a target

Let the ribs stay free, release the jaw, and aim the phrase at one visible point. Do not lift the chin to send sound farther.

2

Flow, then speak

Exhale gently on “vvv,” then keep the same easy airflow as it becomes “very vivid.” Repeat without taking an oversized breath.

3

Clarify the edges

Speak “bright red kite” slowly. Keep final consonants present rather than making the vowel louder. Return to normal pace after three clean repetitions.

Steps 4–6

4. Hum into the phrase

Hum “mm” lightly, open to “many miles,” and notice whether the forward vibration continues. This is a coordination cue, not a demand to place the voice in one exact spot. Keep the hum brief and easy.

5. Use a distance ladder

Say one line to a listener or target at roughly two, four, and six metres. At each distance, keep the words and intention the same. Add only enough energy for intelligibility. If the throat tightens, return to the previous distance and recover an easy version.

6. Add intention, not pressure

Deliver “Meet me by the door” as a welcome, a warning, and an urgent reminder. A clear action often carries better than undirected loudness. Record the three versions and choose the take that sounds most specific without sounding pushed.

A ten-minute practice plan

  1. Two minutes of easy posture, breath, and lip release.
  2. Two minutes of voiced airflow into short words.
  3. Two minutes of crisp consonants at conversation level.
  4. Two minutes on the three-distance ladder.
  5. One minute of contrasting intentions.
  6. One minute of normal-volume recovery.

How to measure progress

Ask the listener to report missed words, not whether the sound felt “big.” When practising alone, place the phone at the destination instead of beside your mouth. Compare word clarity, breath noise, and whether phrase endings remain audible. Keep device gain unchanged so the comparison is useful.

Projection mistakes to avoid

  • Raising the chin or tightening the neck.
  • Taking a breath so large that the shoulders lift.
  • Trading consonant clarity for louder vowels.
  • Starting at the farthest distance.
  • Continuing after pain, persistent roughness, or increasing effort.

Connect projection to performance

Once the phrase carries comfortably, use the articulation routine to sharpen intelligibility or the modulation routine to vary pace, pitch, and intensity. The playable voice imitation game can provide a short scene for an immediate application.

Comfort check

Reach is not strain

  • Easy neck and jaw
  • Steady, unforced breath
  • Clear word endings
  • No maximum-volume calls
  • Recovery at normal speech

Stop if the exercise causes pain, dizziness, increasing hoarseness, or lasting tightness. This is general practice information, not medical care.
