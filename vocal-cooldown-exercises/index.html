The Choicer Voicer/Vocal cooldown exercises

6 minutes · Low intensity · After voice work

Vocal cooldown exercises for an easy return to speaking voice.

A cooldown is a transition, not another workout. Step down from performance energy, release unnecessary effort, make a few comfortable sounds, and finish with ordinary speech. Nothing here should hurt or require extreme range.

Start the six-minute resetSee the pre-session warm-up

Minutes 1–3

Lower the demand gradually.

Use a comfortable middle pitch and quiet-to-conversational volume. The times are guides, not endurance targets.

1

Step down the last line

Repeat the final performance line once at medium energy, once conversationally, and once as simple information. Do not whisper or press for softness.

2

Release movement

Let the shoulders drop, turn the head gently within an easy range, loosen the hands, and allow the jaw to hang without forcing a stretch.

3

Easy breath and hum

Exhale normally, then add a brief, light “mm.” Rest between four repetitions. Use less sound if the hum feels tight or buzzy in an unpleasant way.

Minutes 4–6

4. Small descending slides

On “oo” or a lip trill, slide down through a small comfortable interval. Begin in the middle of your speaking range, not at the top. Complete three or four easy slides with silence between them.

5. Return to ordinary words

Count from one to five, say the day and date, then read one plain sentence at conversational pace. The goal is an unperformed speaking voice with no special character placement, projection, or emotional intensity.

6. Hydrate and log

Take a normal sip of water if available. Write a one-line note: session length, highest demand, and how ordinary speech feels now. A repeated pattern of increasing effort is more useful to notice than a single dramatic label.

Six-minute timing card

  1. 60 seconds: reduce the last performance line.
  2. 60 seconds: easy shoulder, head, hand, and jaw release.
  3. 60 seconds: brief hums with rests.
  4. 60 seconds: small descending slides.
  5. 60 seconds: numbers and ordinary speech.
  6. 60 seconds: water, quiet, and a recovery note.

What a cooldown should not become

  • A test of the highest or lowest note you can make.
  • Repeated throat clearing, forced yawning, or pressed whispering.
  • A way to continue through pain, sudden voice loss, or breathing difficulty.
  • A promise that a general routine can diagnose or fix a persistent problem.

When to stop and seek appropriate help

Stop if sound causes pain, dizziness, shortness of breath, sudden loss of voice, or rapidly increasing effort. Persistent or recurring symptoms deserve guidance from an appropriate qualified clinician. This routine is general educational information and cannot assess an individual condition.

Plan the next session

Before the next performance, use the voice acting warm-up. Reduce avoidable force with breath-control exercises, and use audition exercises to practise ending a recording session on schedule.

Comfort rule

Finish easier than you began

  • Middle range
  • Comfortable volume
  • Small movements
  • Frequent silence
  • Ordinary speech at the end

If an exercise increases effort, skip it. More repetition is not automatically better recovery.
