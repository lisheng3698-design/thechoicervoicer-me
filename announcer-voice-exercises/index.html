6 drills · 18 minutes · Identify before energizing

Announcer voice exercises for accurate, timed delivery.

A useful announcement makes the subject, moment, and next action unmistakable. These drills build concise openings, selective emphasis, reliable timing, calm corrections, and a reset that protects the next line.

Start the timing routineTrain projection

Name it, frame it, land it

Build one announcement in six controlled passes.

Use an original event, station, venue, or program brief. Confirm all names, times, locations, and instructions before recording.

1

Identity opener

Say who or what is being introduced in the first sentence. Remove decorative build-up that delays identification.

2

One-emphasis map

Circle one phrase that must land. Keep surrounding words clear but lighter so the listener can find the priority.

3

Ten-second fit

Deliver the complete message inside ten seconds without crushing endings. Edit the copy before forcing an unsafe pace.

4

Name ladder

Practice the approved name alone, inside a short phrase, and inside the full line. Confirm stress and pronunciation from a reliable source.

5

Correction line

Record a plain correction that names the updated fact once and continues. Do not dramatize or conceal the error.

6

Energy reset

After the final word, release jaw and breath, then begin the next line at its own level instead of carrying the previous peak.

Practice card

Test meaning against the clock

Try this original line: “Welcome to Harbor Hall. Doors open at seven; accessible entry is on Pine Street.” Record it in twelve seconds, then ten, then eight. If a version hides the location or accessibility instruction, it fails even when it meets time.

What should improve on playback?

The subject should arrive immediately, one phrase should carry the emphasis, and every essential fact should remain intelligible. Your loudness should stay comfortable at the recorded distance.

Pass the announcement

Five timing checks

  • Identity arrives first.
  • One priority is emphasized.
  • Names are approved.
  • Time limit is met.
  • Volume stays comfortable.

Pass with four of five; verified facts are mandatory.

Common misses

Three habits that weaken an announcement

Presence is not the same as pressure.

Delayed identity

A long dramatic lead makes listeners guess what the announcement concerns.

Everything is emphasized

Equal weight turns the message into noise and buries the action.

Forced loudness

Shouting into a close microphone reduces control; adjust distance and gain instead.

Continue training

Connect concise delivery to related skills

Use sports commentary voice exercises for live sequencing, voice projection exercises for comfortable presence, and the voice acting exercises game for new prompts.

Do announcers need a deep voice?

No. Clear identification, credible intent, accurate facts, and controlled timing matter more than pitch.

How fast should an announcement be?

Fast enough for the real slot and slow enough for names, numbers, and actions to remain intelligible. Edit copy when those goals conflict.
