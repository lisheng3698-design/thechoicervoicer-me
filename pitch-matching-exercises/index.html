The Choicer Voicer/Pitch matching exercises

7 drills · 10 minutes · No perfect pitch required

Pitch matching exercises for spoken voice.

Start on an easy note, learn the difference between up and down, then trace the shape of a short phrase. The goal is controlled relative movement—not copying someone else's natural register.

Practice with a sceneStart the routine

The routine

Build from one sound to a complete phrase.

Use a piano app, a gentle hum from a partner, or a recording of your own voice as the reference. Stay in a relaxed range.

1

Find a home note

Hum one easy note for three seconds. Rest, replay the reference, and meet it again without sliding from far below.

2

Copy a slow glide

Glide upward on “mm,” then downward. Match the direction, start, turning point, and finish before worrying about precision.

3

Match two points

Alternate a comfortable low and high sound. Keep both sounds easy and make the distance repeatable.

4

Trace a short phrase

Say “ready to begin” level, rising, and falling. Keep the words constant so only the pitch contour changes.

Drills 5–7

5. Question and statement contrast

Say “You finished” as a statement, then as a genuine question. The statement normally settles while the question rises. Record both versions and listen only to the last word. If they sound identical, exaggerate the direction once and then reduce it.

6. Three-point contour

Choose three neutral syllables—“ma, mee, moh.” Perform them low–high–low, then high–low–high. Tap three points on a table as you speak. The visual-spatial cue makes the shape easier to remember than note names.

7. Phrase shadowing

Listen to one short reference line in full. Mark its highest word, lowest word, and final direction. Speak the line in your own comfortable register while preserving those three relationships. Replay both recordings, identify one mismatch, and make one more attempt.

A ten-minute practice schedule

  1. Two minutes: easy home notes with complete rests between attempts.
  2. Two minutes: upward and downward hum glides.
  3. Two minutes: two-point and three-point patterns.
  4. Two minutes: question-versus-statement endings.
  5. Two minutes: one complete phrase with playback.

Stop before fatigue changes your sound. A short accurate session is more useful than repeatedly pushing for a note that sits outside your easy range.

How to review a recording

Ignore vocal beauty and listen for relationships. Did the line begin near the same relative height? Did the main turn happen on the same word? Did the ending rise, fall, or stay level? One clear observation gives the next attempt a usable target.

Use the game as a contour check

The browser game's pitch signal contributes 40% of its similarity model, alongside timing and energy. It compares movement rather than demanding the same identity or natural voice. Common mistakes

  • Chasing an uncomfortable register: transpose the whole pattern into your range.
  • Sliding into every sound: practice a clean start after hearing the reference.
  • Changing pitch and rhythm together: hold the words and timing steady first.
  • Repeating without listening: name one difference before another attempt.

Fast check

Listen for four things

  • Starting height
  • Direction of movement
  • Location of the turn
  • Final rise or fall
