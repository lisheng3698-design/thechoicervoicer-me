The Choicer Voicer/Voice acting character relationship exercises

6 drills · 18 minutes · A real other person

Voice acting character relationship exercises for connected reads.

A line becomes relational when it could only be spoken this way to this person, after this shared history. These drills change familiarity, physical distance, unspoken knowledge, trust, and the listener’s behavior while keeping the actor’s vocal comfort intact.

Start the six-drill routineTrain cue listening

History becomes behavior

Change the bond, then listen for consequences.

Use the original line: “You remembered the blue cup.” Say it to one named imagined listener in every take.

1

Stranger baseline

The listener chose the cup by accident. Record a polite factual take with no shared history.

2

Ten-year history

The cup belonged to a ritual you shared for years. Keep the same words and let recognition alter attention and timing.

3

Distance ladder

Place the listener beside you, across a room, then on a phone line. Adjust focus and privacy without forcing loudness.

4

Shared-secret weight

Only the two characters know why blue matters. Record without illustrating the secret; test whether the line feels protected.

5

Trust turn

Begin expecting betrayal, then realize on “blue” that the listener kept a promise. Mark the exact audible turn.

6

Partner behavior swap

Imagine the listener smiling, avoiding your eyes, then crying silently. Respond to each behavior instead of planning three emotions.

Relationship card

Write facts that can change a take

Before recording, complete four statements: “We last met when…,” “I need them to…,” “They currently believe…,” and “I cannot say….” Concrete facts create playable pressure. Labels such as friend, parent, rival, or boss are only a start; two siblings can carry tenderness, competition, distance, or a new alliance in the same scene.

What should the listener hear?

The recording should imply how much space the characters share, whether information is new, and what response the speaker expects. You do not need a special relationship voice. Small changes in address, pace, breath, and word destination are enough.

Pass the drill

Five connection checks

  • The imagined listener is one person.
  • Shared history is a concrete event.
  • Distance remains deliberate.
  • New behavior changes the response.
  • The take avoids identity stereotypes.

Pass with four of five, including the stereotype check.

Keep it specific

Three choices that erase the partner

Connection is observable when the listener changes what you do.

Playing a label

“Best friend voice” gives no event or goal. Add one remembered moment and a current request.

Addressing an audience

A broad presentation loses private detail. Place one person at a precise distance and send each thought to them.

Ignoring the cue

If every imagined partner behavior produces the same take, the response was planned before listening.

Transfer

Give a game cue a specific relationship

Choose a scene in The Choicer Voicer game and write one shared event that happened before the cue. Use status exercises when permission shifts and reaction exercises when the listener’s behavior changes the next beat.

Can I practice character relationships alone?

Yes. Record a neutral cue or leave timed silence for the partner, then answer a specific imagined behavior. Judge whether your response implies a real other person.

How is relationship work different from subtext?

Relationship defines the shared history and current connection between people. Subtext is the private thought or pressure operating beneath a particular line.

Quick start

One line, three histories

Say the same sentence to a stranger, an old ally, and someone whose trust just changed. Keep the listener specific.
