6 tests · 20 minutes · Change one variable

Voice acting mouth noise exercises for cleaner recordings.

Mouth clicks can be confused with room noise, breath noise, clipping, or aggressive close-mic detail. These exercises use short A/B recordings to locate the audible problem before you change performance, equipment, or editing.

Start the recording testsCheck microphone technique

Record, compare, label

Isolate one noise source in six tests.

Use the same original two-sentence passage for every take. Keep input gain below clipping, listen at a moderate level, and stop if speaking or swallowing is uncomfortable.

1

Room baseline

Record ten seconds of room tone, then the passage. Label hum, traffic, computer fan, and mouth sounds separately.

2

Distance A/B

Compare one marked close position with a slightly greater distance. Match loudness after recording so volume does not bias the decision.

3

Off-axis test

Turn the microphone a small angle while keeping mouth position stable. Check clicks, breaths, consonants, and room tone together.

4

Pace and pause

Repeat the line with complete thought groups and unhurried resets. Do not stretch or tense the jaw to chase silence.

5

Neutral comfort check

Use your normal pre-session routine and compare it with the baseline. Do not use unverified remedies or extreme fluid intake.

6

Edit boundary

Mark clicks that overlap speech versus clicks in gaps. Repair only what you can remove without damaging intelligibility or timing.

A/B sentence

Use a passage that reveals more than one sound

Try: “Mira packed the blue map, paused, and placed it beside the microphone. At noon, the small team would compare every route.” It includes plosives, close consonants, pauses, and word boundaries without borrowing protected dialogue.

What should improve on playback?

You should be able to name where the unwanted sound occurs and which single change helped. A quieter click that creates muffled speech, more room noise, or physical tension is not a passing result.

Pass the test

Five comparison checks

  • Room and mouth sounds are separated.
  • Only one variable changes.
  • Gain does not clip.
  • Speech stays intelligible.
  • No painful effort is used.

Pass with five of five; comfort is mandatory.

Common misses

Three reasons a comparison gives the wrong answer

Control the recording before judging the performer.

Changing everything

New distance, gain, pace, and room in one take make the result impossible to attribute.

Matching by peak only

Unequal playback loudness can make the quieter take seem cleaner even when the noise ratio is unchanged.

Editing through speech

A hard deletion can damage consonants and rhythm more than the original click.

Continue training

Connect diagnosis to microphone and listening skills

Use voice acting microphone technique for repeatable placement, voice acting listening exercises for focused playback, and the voice acting exercises game for fresh lines.

Should I try to remove every mouth sound?

No. Aim for an intelligible, comfortable performance. Some subtle human sound is normal, and destructive editing can make speech less natural.

When should I seek professional advice?

Persistent pain, swallowing difficulty, dryness concerns, or ongoing voice changes require a qualified health professional. This page cannot identify or treat their cause.
