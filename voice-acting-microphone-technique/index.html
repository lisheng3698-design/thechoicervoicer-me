The Choicer Voicer/Voice acting microphone technique

6 tests · One short line · Repeatable position

Voice acting microphone technique you can test and repeat.

A useful microphone position is not a universal number. It is the cleanest distance and angle you can reproduce for your voice, room, microphone, and performance level. Run these controlled comparisons before a longer recording.

Run the six testsFix a microphone problem

Position tests

Change one physical variable at a time.

Use the same line, performance level, input setting, and playback volume. Label each file before moving.

1

Three-distance test

Record at roughly half a hand span, one hand span, and two hand spans. Compare room sound, low-frequency buildup, clarity, and level—not just loudness.

2

Off-axis angle test

Keep your mouth in the same place and compare straight-on, about 20 degrees left, and about 20 degrees right. Choose the cleanest natural tone.

3

Plosive sentence

Say “Bright paper boats pass beneath the bridge.” Listen for bursts on B and P. Adjust angle before moving farther away or changing input level.

4

Quiet-to-loud ladder

Record the same sentence at quiet, conversational, and projected levels. Step back slightly for the projected take while preserving the chosen angle.

5

Head-turn boundary

Speak forward, then turn only enough to address an imagined listener beside you. Mark how far you can move before tone or level changes noticeably.

6

Position recall

Leave the setup for one minute, return, and record again without replaying the earlier take. A repeatable position should produce a similar tone and level.

Setup card

Write down the position you can reproduce

Record microphone height, mouth-to-microphone distance, left or right angle, pop-filter distance, chair or standing position, and the loudness range you tested. A photo can help, but a short written note still works when the room changes.

Set levels after choosing distance

Distance changes tone and room pickup, while input gain changes recorded level. Choose a position first, then set a level that leaves room for the loudest expected phrase. Avoid moving closer to make a quiet waveform look larger; that may also change tone and breath noise.

Keep performance movement intentional

Physical movement can support a scene, but accidental swaying produces level shifts that sound like editing problems. If a character turns, leans in, or calls across a space, rehearse the movement as part of the take and check the result at normal playback volume.

Know what this page does not solve

These tests assume the browser or recording software already receives microphone input. If there is no signal, permission was denied, or the wrong input is selected, follow the microphone troubleshooting guide. Technique cannot repair a disconnected device.

Connect position to performance

Use the voice projection exercises to change vocal energy without simply moving toward the microphone. Then try a line in the browser voice game; microphone analysis remains temporary inside the current tab.

Comparison rule

Do not normalize the files first

Listen once at the recorded levels to notice consistency, then level-match for a second comparison of tone. Keep the original files so the evidence is not lost.

  • Same sentence
  • Same input setting
  • Same performance intention
  • One position change

FAQ

Choose evidence over a universal rule.

Your best position is the cleanest one you can reliably reproduce.

How far away?

Start near one hand span, compare three distances, and label what changes.

Straight or angled?

Compare both. A slight angle often reduces air bursts while preserving clarity.

Why test recall?

A good position should survive a break and still produce a similar recording.
