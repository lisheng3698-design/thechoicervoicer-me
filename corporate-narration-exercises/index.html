The Choicer Voicer/Corporate narration exercises

6 drills · 22 minutes · Approval before polish

Corporate narration exercises for clear, approved business stories.

A credible corporate read guides one defined audience through verified information. This routine protects approved wording, makes hierarchy audible, checks names and numbers, and leaves an unambiguous review trail.

Start the approval-safe routineTrain explainer logic

Audience, fact, action

Record one controlled business message in six passes.

Use an original practice brief or material you are authorized to record. Mark every name, number, claim, qualifier, and call to action before speaking.

1

Audience sentence

Write who is listening, what they already know, and the one decision the read should support. Remove unexplained internal shorthand.

2

Approval lock

Underline approved facts and box wording that cannot change. Put unclear claims in a review list instead of improvising certainty.

3

Hierarchy pass

Give the main message, evidence, qualification, and next step four different levels of emphasis. Do not make every noun sound important.

4

Name and number check

Use an approved pronunciation guide and read dates, percentages, currencies, and version numbers twice against the source.

5

Action landing

Deliver the final action in one complete thought group. Let the listener hear what to do, where to go, and any real condition.

6

Review handoff

Export a take log with script version, open questions, pickups, and exact timecodes. Never hide a guessed correction inside a clean edit.

Practice brief

Make the approval path audible

Try this original line: “Northfield teams will move to the approved portal on October 14; managers receive the checklist one week earlier.” First mark the organization, dates, audience, and action. Then record a neutral information pass and a warmer internal-update pass without changing any fact.

What should improve on playback?

A listener should identify the main change, affected group, date, and next step after one hearing. The second take may change energy and pace, but its facts must match the brief exactly.

Pass the read

Five approval checks

  • Audience is obvious.
  • Approved facts stay exact.
  • Hierarchy is audible.
  • Names and numbers are verified.
  • Review notes include timecodes.

Pass with five of five; factual accuracy is mandatory.

Common misses

Three ways a polished read loses trust

Confidence cannot replace approval.

Invented benefit

Adding “faster,” “safer,” or “industry-leading” changes the claim unless the source explicitly approves it.

Buried qualifier

Rushing a condition or exception changes what a reasonable listener hears.

Untracked pickup

A clean splice without a version and timecode makes final review unreliable.

Should corporate narration always sound formal?

No. Match the audience and approved brand direction. Credibility comes from intelligibility, accurate facts, and consistent intent rather than one permanent tone.

Who approves a factual change?

The named content owner, legal reviewer, or authorized stakeholder for that script. The performer should flag uncertainty, not silently resolve it.
