The Choicer Voicer/Voice acting physicality exercises

6 drills · 18 minutes · Body-to-voice transfer

Voice acting physicality exercises you can hear.

A body choice is useful in voice acting only when it changes the sound, timing, relationship, or effort of the performance. These drills compare one physical variable at a time, then keep the audible result while reducing unnecessary motion.

Start the six-drill routineUse the playback rubric

Move, record, reduce

Test six physical variables without forcing a voice.

Use the same original sentence for every pair of takes: “I kept the light on because I knew you would come back.”

1

Neutral baseline

Stand or sit comfortably, name one listener, and record the line without planning a gesture. This is the comparison take, not a performance to beat.

2

Weight shift

Place your weight slightly forward for pursuit, then slightly back for resistance. Keep volume and microphone distance steady; compare urgency and hesitation.

3

Object resistance

Press your palms gently against an imaginary heavy door while speaking, then release. Listen for changed consonants, breath timing, and effort—not louder sound.

4

Gesture endpoint

Give one operative word a clear hand destination. Record again with a smaller version of the gesture and check whether the word still lands.

5

Listener distance

Place the imagined listener one step away, across a room, then behind a closed door. Change intention and focus while keeping a safe recording level.

6

Stillness transfer

Return to neutral posture and repeat the most useful take. Keep only the breath, timing, and thought changes that remain audible without visible movement.

Three-take protocol

Separate cause from decoration

  1. Take A—neutral: record the sentence with a specific listener and no assigned movement.
  2. Take B—contrast: change one physical variable enough to make the difference easy to identify.
  3. Take C—reduced: shrink the movement by half while preserving the useful audible effect.

Label the files with the variable rather than an emotion. “Forward weight” gives you a repeatable cause; “more intense” does not explain what changed. If Take B introduces breath holding, strain, unstable mic distance, or clothing noise, stop and choose a smaller action.

What should change on playback?

Listen for the start of the line, phrase length, operative word, pause placement, ending, and relationship to the listener. One meaningful difference is enough. A bigger gesture is not automatically a clearer performance.

Pass the drill

Five audible checks

  • The physical variable is named before recording.
  • Only one variable changes between takes.
  • The listener relationship remains specific.
  • No strain, bump, or accidental level jump appears.
  • The reduced take preserves one useful difference.

Pass with four of five, including the safety and noise check.

Clean comparisons

Three traps that hide the useful signal

Physicality supports behavior; it does not replace listening or text work.

Changing everything

A new posture, accent, tempo, pitch, and volume create an exciting take but reveal nothing about which choice helped.

Performing for a mirror

If the movement looks expressive but the recording sounds unchanged, remove it or give it a clearer task and endpoint.

Chasing effort

Muscular effort can read as tension rather than stakes. Use imagined resistance lightly and release it immediately after the line.

Transfer

Bring one body choice into a playable scene

Choose a cue in The Choicer Voicer game. Listen for the reference performance, name one physical cause that could support its timing, and record a comfortable take. Then use microphone technique to keep distance deliberate and self critique to compare evidence.

Does physicality matter when nobody can see the actor?

Yes when it changes breath timing, focus, distance, resistance, or intention in a way the listener can hear. Movement that produces no useful audible change can be removed.

Should a voice actor use large movements at the microphone?

Begin with a safe exaggerated contrast away from the microphone, then reduce it until the audible choice remains without bumps, clothing noise, or changing mic distance accidentally.

Quick start

One sentence, three takes

Record neutral, change one body variable, then reduce the movement. Keep the smallest version that creates a clear audible result.
