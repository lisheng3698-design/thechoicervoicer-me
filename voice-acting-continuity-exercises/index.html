Voice Acting Continuity Exercises

Reconstruct a character performance after a break or out-of-order recording. Keep a private reference for relationship, pace, energy, and recording position so a later take belongs to the same scene.

Start the six-step routineRead the original example

Controlled practice

Work through the routine one step at a time.

Change one variable, listen back, then mark the step. Progress stays on this device and carries across the language switch. The checklist stores completion flags, never recordings.

1

Make a continuity card

Note listener, objective, emotional starting point, and recording position for the sample. Use observable details rather than only the word intense.

2

Save the anchor take

Record the first line and a brief lead-in. Keep this reference available for a later session instead of relying on memory.

3

Resume after a short break

Step away briefly, replay the anchor, and record the next line. Compare phrase endings and energy before changing anything.

4

Record out of order

Record the last line first, then the middle line. Use the card to reconstruct the starting state of each line.

5

Assemble the sequence

Play the three lines in story order. Identify any sudden change in relationship, distance, or pace that the scene does not justify.

6

Repair one mismatch

Rerecord only the line with a documented mismatch. Compare it against both neighboring lines, then update the continuity note.

0 of 6 steps completed. Self-review, not an audio score.

Original practice card

Use one fixed example for a fair comparison.

Line 1: The bridge is still closed. Line 2: We can wait beside the marker. Line 3: When the signal changes, we move together. One companion reassures another; the relationship remains steady across all three lines.

How should the first pass work?

Read the original example and limits, then work through the six steps using your own notes and any equipment required by the task. Keep a baseline or current version and check the stated result before marking a step complete. The checklist records your progress locally; it does not inspect files, judge audio, or perform the task for you.

Observable result

Check the result against the criteria.

In a blind playback, a listener should not detect a session break from an unexplained character or recording change. Intentional story changes should remain audible and be documented separately.

A checked box records that you completed a step. It does not certify voice quality, skill, or professional readiness.

Limits and failure cases

Repair a failed comparison before continuing.

The checklist does not automatically compare waveforms, match voices, or repair edits. It targets scene continuity; exact pickup audio specifications need a separate technical check.

Too many simultaneous changes

Changing words, level, position, and pace together makes the comparison inconclusive. Return to the example baseline and retain only the variable specified by the current step.

Impressions instead of evidence

A more attractive sound does not establish that the task worked. Apply the page-specific playback checks, and ask a listener unfamiliar with the example to recount the information before keeping a take.

Effort used to force completion

The completion record is not an endurance contest. Stop at discomfort, shorten the session, and return to comfortable speech; pain or excessive vocal effort is never a condition for checking a box.
