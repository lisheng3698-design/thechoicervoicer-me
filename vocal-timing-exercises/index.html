The Choicer Voicer/Vocal timing exercises

7 drills · 12 minutes · Spoken rhythm

Vocal timing exercises for clean, deliberate speech.

Train the moment you enter, the space you leave, and the moment you finish. These drills isolate spoken timing so you can hear whether a phrase is early, late, rushed, or evenly shaped.

First four drills

Make time visible before adding expression.

Use one short sentence throughout: “We begin after the light.” Record each drill once, listen, then repeat with one change.

1

Three-count entry

Count one, two, three silently and start exactly on an imagined fourth beat. Avoid inhaling after the start point.

2

Pause placement

Say the line with a pause after “begin,” then after “the.” Compare how the location changes meaning and flow.

3

Duration target

Speak the line naturally, note its length, and repeat within roughly half a second while staying relaxed.

4

Tap the syllables

Tap only the stressed syllables. Speak again while keeping those anchor points equally spaced.

Drills 5–7

5. Slow–quick–slow

Divide a sentence into three thought groups. Give the first room, move through the middle with energy, and let the final group settle. Repeat with the reverse pattern. The point is not raw speed; it is choosing where pace changes happen.

6. Cue and response

Ask a partner to clap once at an unpredictable moment. Enter one beat after the clap, deliver the phrase, and stop cleanly. Working alone, schedule a repeating tone and use every second tone as the cue. This separates preparation from a premature start.

7. Reference shadowing

Listen to one short recording without speaking over it. Mark the start, longest pause, and finish with three pencil strokes. Perform after the reference ends. Compare the two recordings and adjust only the largest timing difference.

A twelve-minute schedule

  1. Two minutes of silent counts and clean entries.
  2. Two minutes moving one deliberate pause.
  3. Two minutes matching total phrase duration.
  4. Two minutes tapping stressed syllables.
  5. Two minutes changing pace by thought group.
  6. Two minutes shadowing one complete reference.

How to diagnose the playback

Use a simple timeline: start, first stressed word, longest pause, final word, finish. If your take feels rushed, determine whether the issue is an early start, missing pause, compressed middle, or abrupt finish. “Too fast” is vague; “the middle loses one pause” is actionable.

Timing inside The Choicer Voicer

The game gives three visible preparation lights and a silent fourth start signal. Wait for that start, preserve the reference's major pause, and finish near its ending. Timing contributes 25% of the comparison, while energy and pitch remain separate signals. Use the step-by-step controls guide if you need the complete round sequence.

Frequent timing problems

  • Entering on the preparation light: rehearse the silent count before recording.
  • Matching every syllable mechanically: anchor stressed words and let unstressed words flow.
  • Removing pauses to sound fluent: keep pauses that organize meaning.
  • Speeding up after a mistake: finish the phrase at the chosen pace, then review.
  • Changing the script each attempt: keep words fixed while training timing.

Timing practice works best at a normal speaking level. If the recording has gaps or the input meter stays still, solve the device issue with the microphone troubleshooting page first.
