The Choicer Voicer/Voice acting line delivery exercises

6 variables · One line · Audible comparison

Voice acting line delivery exercises with one change at a time.

A useful retake is not simply “more energy” or “different.” Hold the words and situation steady, change one delivery control, then decide which version best serves the listener.

Run the six controlsPractice voice modulation

Controlled contrast

Build six versions of the same short line.

Use: “I left the note beside the blue cup.” Name the listener and action before each take.

1

Entry

Begin once after a full thought and once as if answering an interruption. Compare how much urgency exists before the first word.

2

Operative word

Stress “note,” then “beside,” then “blue.” State what correction each version gives the listener.

3

Pause

Place one pause before the location, then before the object. Keep its purpose—remembering, checking, or making sure—clear.

4

Pace

Use a calm clarification and a time-critical warning. Preserve consonants and the final destination in both versions.

5

Ending

Land the last word as reassurance, correction, or a private realization. Avoid automatically dropping energy at the period.

6

Listener distance

Speak to someone beside you, across a desk, and through a closed door. Change direction and clarity without forcing volume.

Delivery matrix

Connect every variable to a reason

Create two columns: control and effect on the listener. “Pause before blue” is incomplete; “pause before blue so they replace their wrong assumption” is playable. Record the line, fill the matrix from what you hear, and keep only changes that support the intended effect.

The two-take rule

Make only two versions before listening. Too many unlabelled takes turn review into guesswork. Name the variable, compare, select, and then move to the next control.

Playback review

Ask four exact questions

  • Which word carries the correction?
  • Does the pause contain a thought?
  • Can every word survive the pace?
  • Does the ending affect the listener?

If the answer is unclear, change the action first and record one more take.

Twelve-minute session

Use a fixed line as a small laboratory

Record two entries, two operative-word choices, two pauses, two paces, two endings, and two listener distances. Keep each pair under a minute. Spend the remaining time choosing one version from each pair and explaining the listener effect in a sentence.

Transfer without copying sound

When you move to a new script, transfer the decision process rather than the exact melody. Find the new listener, action, and key word. Then choose the smallest delivery control that clarifies them. In the voice imitation game, listen for the reference entry and ending before making your own take.

Starter line

“I left the note beside the blue cup.”

Keep the words fixed. Change entry, key word, pause, pace, ending, then distance—in that order.
