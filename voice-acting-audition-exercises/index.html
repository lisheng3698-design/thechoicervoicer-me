The Choicer Voicer/Voice acting audition exercises

20 minutes · Timed preparation · Two useful takes

Voice acting audition exercises for calm, clear decisions.

Rehearse the part you can control: reading the brief, marking the copy, making a playable choice, recording two purposeful takes, and stopping before endless repetition erases the performance.

Round 1 · Six minutes

Prepare without overplanning.

Use any 15–25 second script from this site. Set a timer so the exercise tests decisions, not unlimited editing.

1

Read the brief

Write four facts: audience, objective, tone boundary, and target length. Separate supplied direction from assumptions you invented.

2

Mark the turn

Underline the operative words, mark one thought change, and draw a slash at a natural breath. Avoid marking every word as important.

3

Cold read once

Read aloud without recording. Fix only meaning errors, hard pronunciations, and breath placement; do not polish a final performance yet.

Rounds 2–3

4. Record take one for the brief

Choose a specific listener and action. Record the copy once from beginning to end, then leave ten seconds of silence before playback. Do not interrupt a useful take because one syllable felt imperfect.

5. Design one contrast

Change only one meaningful dimension for take two: closer listener, less urgency, more private stakes, faster discovery, or a different tactic. Keep the format, pronunciation, and target length consistent unless the brief says otherwise.

6. Record take two, then stop

Record the contrasting version. Limiting the drill to two takes makes the choice legible and prevents a rehearsal from becoming an editing marathon. A later session can use different copy.

7. Practise a clean slate

If a slate is requested in your practice scenario, say only the information specified by that scenario in a natural voice. Rehearse separating it from the performance with a short pause. Real submission requirements vary, so follow the directions you actually receive.

A five-point playback rubric

  1. Brief: does the delivery fit the stated audience and boundary?
  2. Meaning: can the listener follow the thought and its turn?
  3. Choice: is the action specific rather than merely energetic?
  4. Contrast: does take two offer a useful alternative?
  5. Sound: is speech clear, comfortable, and free of distracting handling noise?

Technical rehearsal without equipment claims

Use the quietest practical location, keep the microphone position steady, record a short room sample, and listen for clipping or heavy background noise. This page does not recommend a particular device, file format, agent, platform, or delivery specification; those depend on the brief.

Reset after the review

Save one concrete lesson, close the recording, and do a gentle vocal cooldown. For fixed copy use the practice script library; for responsive choices use improv exercises.

Scope

Rehearsal, not a casting promise

This exercise builds a repeatable preparation process. It does not submit an audition, represent a casting platform, evaluate employability, or guarantee work.
