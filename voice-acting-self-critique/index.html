The Choicer Voicer/Voice acting self critique

5 categories · 0–2 score · One-variable retake

Voice acting self critique without vague “good” or “bad” notes.

A useful review describes what can be heard and gives the next take one job. Pause after recording, listen for one category at a time, then change the highest-impact variable instead of judging your whole voice at once.

Use the five-part rubricRecord a practice take

Playback pass

Score behavior you can hear.

Use 0 for missing, 1 for inconsistent, and 2 for clear. Write one timestamp or phrase as evidence.

1

Intention

Can you identify the listener and the action from sound alone? Note the moment the relationship becomes clear or disappears.

2

Clarity

Can a listener follow every important word without seeing the script? Record any swallowed ending, blurred transition, or over-shaped consonant.

3

Timing

Do pauses separate thoughts, and does the ending arrive deliberately? Mark one rushed phrase, empty gap, or well-shaped change of pace.

4

Vocal contrast

Do pitch, pace, intensity, and resonance respond to meaning? Name one useful contrast and one place where the pattern becomes repetitive.

5

Recording consistency

Listen for room changes, air bursts, clipping, clothing noise, or distance shifts. Separate technical problems from performance choices.

Four-pass review

Listen in an order that reduces bias

  1. First pass—meaning: look away from the script and summarize what happened. Do not take detailed notes yet.
  2. Second pass—structure: follow the text and mark intention, clarity, and timing evidence.
  3. Third pass—sound: listen only for vocal contrast and recording consistency.
  4. Fourth pass—decision: choose one change that can improve the whole read or remove its largest distraction.

Turn judgment into an actionable note

Replace “flat” with “the last three sentences use the same pace and pitch ending.” Replace “unclear” with “the instruction after 00:14 loses its final consonants.” Replace “too dramatic” with “every operative word receives the same intensity, so the main turn has no contrast.” Each rewritten note points to a testable change.

Use a one-variable retake

Keep the listener, distance, input setting, and most performance choices stable. Change only the selected variable, such as slowing the turn, finishing word endings, reducing emphasis, or holding microphone position. Compare the two takes at similar playback levels, then record one sentence about what improved and what did not.

Protect the value of the first take

Do not overwrite the baseline. Label files with date, script, take number, and intended change. The purpose is not to collect perfect files; it is to make progress visible and prevent a later mood from rewriting what actually happened.

Choose the next exercise from evidence

For an unclear intention, return to voice acting script analysis. For repetitive vocal patterns, use voice modulation exercises. For recording inconsistency, run the microphone technique tests.

FAQ

Critique the take, not your identity.

Observable notes create options; global labels close them.

How do I stay fair?

Wait briefly, use one category at a time, and cite an audible moment.

What should change?

Choose the single adjustment most likely to clarify meaning or remove distraction.

Keep the baseline?

Yes. A saved first take lets you verify improvement instead of relying on memory.
