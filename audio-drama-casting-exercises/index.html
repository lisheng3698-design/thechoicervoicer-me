Audio Drama Casting Exercises

Compare roles by scene objectives and relationships rather than surface voice novelty. Use an original two-person audio scene to prepare matching audition sides and a repeatable listening rubric for a small casting rehearsal.

Start the six-step routineRead the original example

Controlled practice

Work through the routine one step at a time.

Change one variable, listen back, then mark the step. Progress stays on this device and carries across the language switch. The checklist stores completion flags, never recordings.

1

Write the role needs

Define each character’s objective, listener, and practical constraints. Avoid requirements that the script cannot demonstrate.

2

Use the same audition side

Give every rehearsal reader the same lines and context. Keep direction and text version consistent so comparisons are fair.

3

Test a contrasting relationship

Read the scene once as colleagues and once as strangers. Listen for an audible change in trust rather than a cosmetic pitch shift.

4

Assess without seeing names

Label rehearsal files A and B for an initial listening pass. Record intelligibility, intention, and response to new information before discussing preference.

5

Test a callback note

Give one specific direction and record a second take. Check whether the reader can apply it while retaining the scene facts.

6

Review the pairing

Listen to the two roles in sequence. Verify distinct intentions, coherent responses, and consistent names before selecting a rehearsal pairing.

0 of 6 steps completed. Self-review, not an audio score.

Original practice card

Use one fixed example for a fair comparison.

Keeper: The lantern is missing. Courier: I left it beside the east door. Keeper: Then someone moved it after you arrived. Context: the keeper needs the truth; the courier needs to avoid a false accusation.

How should the first pass work?

Read the original example and limits, then work through the six steps using your own notes and any equipment required by the task. Keep a baseline or current version and check the stated result before marking a step complete. The checklist records your progress locally; it does not inspect files, judge audio, or perform the task for you.

Observable result

Check the result against the criteria.

The listener should track who needs what and hear the response to the new fact. Compare readers on the stated role needs; an unusual voice alone does not establish a fit.

A checked box records that you completed a step. It does not certify voice quality, skill, or professional readiness.

Limits and failure cases

Repair a failed comparison before continuing.

This is a casting rehearsal, not an audition marketplace or employment decision system. Obtain permission before sharing recordings and use actual production requirements for real casting.

Too many simultaneous changes

Changing words, level, position, and pace together makes the comparison inconclusive. Return to the example baseline and retain only the variable specified by the current step.

Impressions instead of evidence

A more attractive sound does not establish that the task worked. Apply the page-specific playback checks, and ask a listener unfamiliar with the example to recount the information before keeping a take.

Effort used to force completion

The completion record is not an endurance contest. Stop at discomfort, shorten the session, and return to comfortable speech; pain or excessive vocal effort is never a condition for checking a box.
