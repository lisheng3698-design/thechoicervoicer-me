Nine party formats

1. Same-scene showdown

Each person hears the same reference and performs it once. The Choicer Voicer Group mode handles player order, four scenes, five judge profiles, and a final local leaderboard. It supports 2–4 named players on one shared device; it does not create a remote lobby.

2. Imitation telephone

The first player invents a three-second voice phrase and whispers or records it for the next player. Each person imitates only the version they received. Compare the first and last performance. Award a point for the detail that survived longest.

3. Emotion switch

Choose a harmless sentence such as “The parcel arrived this morning.” A caller names two emotions. The performer begins with one and switches at a marked word. Listeners guess the exact switch point.

4. Sound-effect scene

Give a team a location—train station, forest, kitchen, or spacecraft—and thirty seconds to build it using only voices. The other team identifies the place and names the most useful sound clue.

5. Mystery voice

Listeners close their eyes while one person changes pace, energy, and pitch. Guess the performer after one sentence. Keep impressions fictional or generic and avoid targeting protected personal traits.

6. Silent director

A director may communicate only with hand signals: louder, quieter, faster, slower, higher, lower, stop. The performer reads a short neutral line while following the live directions. Swap roles after thirty seconds.

7. One-word character

Write familiar character types on cards—sleepy navigator, excited inventor, nervous detective, calm coach. A player draws one and says only “hello.” The group guesses the type, then explains which timing, energy, or pitch clue helped.

8. Rhythm copy chain

Tap and speak a short pattern using nonsense syllables. The next player copies it and adds one beat. Continue until the chain breaks, then reset with a shorter pattern so eliminated players rejoin immediately.

9. Caption dubbing

Show a muted, rights-cleared clip or use an imaginary scene description. Pairs invent a ten-second exchange, rehearse once, and perform it live. Score clarity, commitment, and teamwork instead of production polish.

A host plan for twenty minutes

  1. Two minutes: explain that joining a performance is optional.
  2. Three minutes: cooperative sound-effect scene.
  3. Five minutes: emotion switch in pairs.
  4. Eight minutes: Group mode or same-scene showdown.
  5. Two minutes: let the room choose a rematch or a new format.

Make the room comfortable

Keep clips short, never pressure a guest to reproduce a real person's accent, and let anyone choose a judging or directing role. Use a normal speaking level. If the microphone is part of the game, test it before gathering attention and use headphones when reference audio might echo.
