The Choicer Voicer/Voice acting objective exercises

6 drills · 18 minutes · Listener-facing action

Voice acting objective exercises for playable goals.

An objective is not “sound confident” or “be sad.” It is the change you want from one listener. This routine keeps a short line stable while you name a result, meet resistance, change tactics, and judge success by the listener response you can hear in your imagination.

Start the objective routineMark the script first

Want something specific

Build the objective from listener to outcome.

Use the original line: “Please leave the blue folder by the door.” Keep the words, comfortable voice, and microphone distance constant.

1

Name the listener

Choose one real relationship and describe what the listener believes before you speak. Record a neutral information-only baseline.

2

Write the result

Finish “I need them to…” with an observable response: comply, trust me, stop asking, or protect the folder. Record toward that result.

3

Add an obstacle

Assume the listener distrusts you, is distracted, or wants the folder elsewhere. Let resistance change your timing without inventing new words.

4

Change the tactic

Try reassure, recruit, warn, and challenge. Each verb must alter one audible behavior rather than merely raise intensity.

5

Hear the feedback

Imagine the listener softens after “blue” or resists after “folder.” Pivot at that word and complete the line with the new evidence.

6

Blind outcome test

Shuffle three tactic takes. Ask a listener to name what response each speaker wanted, then keep the clearest take rather than the biggest one.

Objective map

Separate script marking from performance practice

Script analysis locates the listener, beats, operative words, and turn. Objective practice tests what you actually do with those marks. Write one outward action above the line and one obstacle below it. If the action cannot affect another person, replace it with a more playable verb.

What should change on playback?

The start should reveal whether you expect cooperation, the middle should register resistance, and the ending should show whether the tactic continues or changes. Pitch and volume may move, but only as consequences of the attempt.

Pass the drill

Five objective checks

  • The listener is one specific person.
  • The desired response is observable.
  • The action verb can affect the listener.
  • The obstacle changes behavior.
  • The pivot responds to new evidence.

Pass with four of five, including the observable response.

Avoid dead goals

Three choices that cannot be played

A useful objective creates a live exchange, even when you record alone.

Wanting a feeling

“Be nervous” points inward. “Prevent them from opening the folder” gives the listener and every word a job.

Playing the result

Sounding victorious before the listener responds removes uncertainty. Attempt the action and leave room for resistance.

Using volume as a tactic

Louder is a measurement, not an action. Warn, invite, dismiss, bargain, or reassure before touching the level.

Transfer

Put the objective inside a responsive scene

Choose a cue from The Choicer Voicer game, use character relationship exercises to define why the listener matters, then use subtext exercises when the literal line must hide the real request. Review whether the tactic changes with self critique.

What is a playable objective in voice acting?

It is a result you want from a specific listener, expressed as an action you can attempt during the line, such as reassure, recruit, delay, or challenge.

How is an objective different from an emotion?

An objective points outward toward changing the listener, while emotion describes an inner condition. Pursuing the objective may produce emotion without forcing a sound.
