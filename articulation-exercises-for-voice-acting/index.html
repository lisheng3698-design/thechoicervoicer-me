The Choicer Voicer/Articulation exercises for voice acting

8 drills · Clear words · Expressive speech

Articulation exercises for voice acting, from sounds to scenes.

Train intelligibility without flattening emotion or accent. This ladder starts with clean sound pairs, adds endings and clusters, restores natural pace, and finishes with a short acted line.

Begin the articulation ladderTry a scene line

Drills 1–4

Make precise movement before adding speed.

Use a mirror only to observe obvious tension. The jaw, lips, and tongue should stay active but not rigid.

1

Lip pairs

Alternate “pay–bay,” “fee–vee,” and “may–way.” Keep each first sound distinct while the following vowel stays easy.

2

Tongue-tip pairs

Speak “tea–dee,” “see–zee,” and “lay–ray” slowly. Use the smallest movement that makes the contrast clear.

3

Stable vowels

Repeat “meet–met–mat–mutt–moot.” Avoid spreading every vowel into the same shape as the pace increases.

4

Word endings

Contrast “cap–cab,” “seat–seed,” and “pick–pig.” Let the final sound be audible without adding an extra vowel.

Drills 5–8

5. Consonant clusters

Break “bright street,” “asked twice,” and “old script” into two parts, join them slowly, then return to conversational pace. Do not punch every consonant equally; protect the sounds that carry the word identity.

6. Three-speed ladder

Use “Fresh green grapes grow quickly.” Speak it at half speed, normal speed, and a slightly faster pace. Record all three. The fastest useful version is the last one in which every word remains easy to understand.

7. Thought-group marking

Mark a slash where the thought turns: “After the lights went out / we heard the gate.” Speak each group as one connected idea. Clear phrasing prevents over-articulation from turning the line into isolated words.

8. Restore the action

Deliver the same line while warning, reassuring, and distracting someone. Keep the consonants available but let the intention lead. If the acted version becomes muddy, reduce the pace rather than exaggerating mouth movement.

A ten-minute daily set

  1. One minute: relaxed lip and tongue movement.
  2. Two minutes: sound pairs.
  3. One minute: vowel distinctions.
  4. Two minutes: final consonants and clusters.
  5. Two minutes: three-speed phrase.
  6. Two minutes: acted line and playback.

Listen for useful evidence

On playback, write down the exact word that disappeared or changed. Repeat only that word group, then place it back into the whole sentence. A vague instruction such as “speak more clearly” is harder to apply than “the final t in ‘left’ disappeared at fast pace.”

Keep clarity natural

Articulation is not the same as adopting one accent. Preserve the vowel and rhythm choices that belong to the speaker or character unless the scene calls for a different pattern. The target is intentional, intelligible delivery. Avoid sharp jaw tension, oversized facial movement, and endless high-speed tongue twisters.

Build the next layer

When individual words are clear, use voice modulation exercises to vary meaning or projection drills to carry those words farther. For a gentle starting sequence, begin with the eight-minute warm-up.

Clarity check

Precise, not rigid

  • Active lips and tongue
  • Released jaw
  • Audible word endings
  • Natural thought groups
  • Meaning still leads

Stop any drill that causes pain or increasing tension. This page gives general practice guidance rather than medical or speech therapy.
