Voice Over Recording Room Checklist

Compare a room’s background noise and audible reflections using a repeatable test. Check your current recording position before moving furniture or buying treatment; use saved files to distinguish outside noise from room reflections.

Start the six-step routineRead the original example

Controlled practice

Work through the routine one step at a time.

Change one variable, listen back, then mark the step. Progress stays on this device and carries across the language switch. The checklist stores completion flags, never recordings.

1

Log the normal conditions

Note time, open doors or windows, and nearby appliances. Test during the conditions in which the real session will occur.

2

Record quiet room tone

Keep the microphone and gain fixed and save a short quiet segment. Listen for intermittent traffic, fans, or neighboring activity rather than assuming silence.

3

Record a fixed sentence

Use the same sample line and position for every test. Leave a short pause after it so the decay and background can be heard.

4

Change one placement

Move the recording position once while keeping settings consistent. Compare the two files for reflections and noise, not just louder output.

5

Inspect practical interruptions

Check chair noise, clothing movement, desk vibration, and notifications. Test a natural movement while listening to the resulting recording.

6

Retest the chosen setup

Repeat the full line and quiet segment in the selected position. Record remaining limitations and check them with the receiving engineer when needed.

0 of 6 steps completed. Self-review, not an audio score.

Original practice card

Use one fixed example for a fair comparison.

Room test: The paper lantern rests beside the doorway. Pause, then repeat at the second position using the same input settings. File A = original position; file B = changed position; write the time and observable difference.

How should the first pass work?

Read the original example and limits, then work through the six steps using your own notes and any equipment required by the task. Keep a baseline or current version and check the stated result before marking a step complete. The checklist records your progress locally; it does not inspect files, judge audio, or perform the task for you.

Observable result

Check the result against the criteria.

Choose the position where the words remain clear and unwanted sound is reduced consistently in the repeated test. A single quiet moment does not prove the room is suitable throughout a session.

A checked box records that you completed a step. It does not certify voice quality, skill, or professional readiness.

Limits and failure cases

Repair a failed comparison before continuing.

No room measurement, soundproofing rating, acoustic certification, or material recommendation is provided. Do not obstruct ventilation or make structural changes to complete this checklist; consult qualified help for building work.

Too many simultaneous changes

Changing words, level, position, and pace together makes the comparison inconclusive. Return to the example baseline and retain only the variable specified by the current step.

Impressions instead of evidence

A more attractive sound does not establish that the task worked. Apply the page-specific playback checks, and ask a listener unfamiliar with the example to recount the information before keeping a take.

Effort used to force completion

The completion record is not an endurance contest. Stop at discomfort, shorten the session, and return to comfortable speech; pain or excessive vocal effort is never a condition for checking a box.
