The Choicer Voicer/Explainer video voice over exercises

6 drills · 20 minutes · One clear causal path

Explainer video voice over exercises that make ideas click.

An explainer works when a viewer can name the problem, follow what changes, and take the next action. These exercises coordinate voice with pictures, give each beat one reasoning job, and test understanding instead of rewarding generic enthusiasm.

Start the explainer routineMap the script

Show plus tell

Give every beat one useful job.

Practice with an original idea: “The dashboard groups today’s tasks, shows what is blocked, and lets you assign the next step.”

1

Problem in one breath

State who is stuck and why in one comfortable phrase. Remove scene-setting that does not change the problem.

2

Causal ladder

Mark “because,” “so,” and “therefore,” even when implied. Record one mechanism step per thought group.

3

Picture handoff

Cross out words that merely name visible objects. Use the voice to explain purpose, sequence, or consequence.

4

Action landing

Place the call to action after the viewer has enough context. Make the verb audible without turning the line into a sales shout.

5

Timed storyboard

Read against six blank frames. Let each verbal beat land inside its frame and leave transitions enough room to register.

6

Teach-back test

Play audio once without visuals. Ask a listener to state the problem, mechanism, and next action; repair the first missing link.

Explanation map

Preserve the chain, not every adjective

Label each sentence problem, mechanism, proof, action, or result. If a line does two jobs, decide which belongs in narration and which belongs on screen. Product names and interface labels stay exact; decorative claims can go.

What should change on playback?

The viewer should hear why one step leads to the next. Energy may rise at the reveal or action, but the explanation must remain understandable at ordinary conversational volume.

Pass the explainer

Five clarity checks

  • The audience problem is specific.
  • Every causal step is audible.
  • Voice and picture do different work.
  • The next action arrives on time.
  • A listener can teach it back.

Pass with four of five, including the causal path.

Common misses

Three ways a polished explainer becomes unclear

Clarity is measured by recovery, not brightness.

Reading the picture

Naming every icon duplicates the visual and leaves no space to explain why it matters.

Skipping the mechanism

Jumping from problem to benefit creates a claim, not an explanation. Give the viewer the missing “how.”

Permanent excitement

Equal enthusiasm flattens hierarchy. Save emphasis for the turn, result, or next action.

Related practice

Connect the explanation to adjacent skills

Use e-learning narration exercises when the viewer must complete a procedure, commercial voice acting exercises when persuasion becomes primary, taking-direction exercises for revisions, and The Choicer Voicer game for quick contrast takes.

How conversational should an explainer voice over sound?

Sound like one informed person guiding one viewer. Keep causal links and approved product terms clearer than any stylistic flourish.

Should narration describe everything on screen?

No. Let the picture carry visible detail while the voice supplies purpose, sequence, or meaning the viewer cannot get from the image alone.

Quick start

Problem, mechanism, action

Record those three beats. If a listener cannot connect them, repair the first missing cause.
