The Choicer Voicer/ADR voice acting exercises

6 drills · 22 minutes · Original material only

ADR voice acting exercises for sync and continuity.

ADR asks you to enter on time, fit visible action, preserve the original scene job, and sound as if the line was never replaced. These exercises use a timer, your own recordings, and original text—never an unlicensed commercial scene.

Start the ADR routineStabilize mic position

Hit the cue, keep the thought

Train sync without flattening the performance.

Use the original line: “Wait—the train leaves before sunrise.” Record a reference take before attempting any timing fit.

1

Reference take

Record the line freely with a clear listener, objective, and turn. Measure its duration without trying to fit a target.

2

Three-beep entry

Tap three evenly spaced cues and begin where an imaginary fourth tap would land. Repeat until the first word enters consistently.

3

Duration window

Fit the line into 2.8, 2.4, then 3.2 seconds by changing thought pickup and pauses—not by swallowing consonants.

4

Closure anchors

Mark “Wait,” “train,” and “sunrise” as visible mouth or action anchors. Land the key consonants without stretching every syllable.

5

Context continuity

Imagine the reference was recorded while walking, sitting, or turning. Match breath state, distance, energy, and listener position.

6

Pickup splice test

Replace only “before sunrise.” Play the full line and judge whether tone, room, pace, and intention cross the edit invisibly.

ADR map

Track four kinds of continuity

Write timing, vocal condition, physical action, and dramatic intention in four boxes. A technically exact entry can still fail if the pickup sounds like a new character or a different moment. Match the scene job first, then improve the smallest sync mismatch.

Three representative tests

Test an early entry, a late entry, and a correct entry with weak intention. The best take must meet both timing and performance criteria; neither one cancels the other.

Pass the loop

Five ADR checks

  • The entry lands after the third cue.
  • Duration fits without lost words.
  • Closure anchors remain clear.
  • Breath and distance match context.
  • The pickup preserves intention.

Pass with four of five, including the intact words.

Protect the scene

Three sync mistakes to avoid

Precise timing is useful only when the line still belongs to the moment.

Chasing every mouth shape

Prioritize entry, exit, key closures, and visible actions. Overfitting every vowel can make the speech unnatural.

Compressing articulation

Do not erase consonants to meet duration. Pick up the thought sooner, simplify pauses, or ask whether the wording can change.

Using an unlicensed scene

Practice with self-recorded, public-domain, or expressly authorized material. Do not repost or copy protected dialogue.

Transfer

Keep timing, memory, and reaction in one loop

Build a short original cue in The Choicer Voicer game, use memory exercises to preserve pickup facts, and use reaction exercises so the thought changes before the mouth moves. Check level and angle with microphone technique.

Can I practice ADR without a professional video loop?

Yes. Use a timer, three evenly spaced taps, and your own recorded video or other material you are authorized to use. Timing and continuity can be trained without copying a commercial scene.

What is the difference between ADR and dubbing?

ADR usually replaces or improves dialogue for the same production and performance context. Dubbing often creates a new-language performance that must also fit timing and visible action.

Quick start

Three taps, one entry

Tap at equal intervals and start the train line on the silent fourth beat. Preserve the listener and objective.
