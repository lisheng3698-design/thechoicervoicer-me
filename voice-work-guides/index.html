Voice Work Guides: Rehearsal, Recording & Delivery

Explore 15 practical voice-work guides for bilingual rehearsals, demo preparation, studio setup, directed sessions, pronunciation, and file delivery.

Choose by the task in front of you

Prepare the script and performance, check the recording setup and session, then verify delivery. Each page tracks self-review progress; completion flags do not replace recording, file inspection, or client approval.

Bilingual Voice Acting Exercises

Keep a character intention recognizable across two languages. Compare meaning, register, names, and scene timing with a paired English and Chinese practice card, rather than treating literal translation as a finished performance.

Voice Over Demo Reel Exercises

Build and review a small rehearsal sequence that shows distinct performance choices. Use original scenes, clear transitions, and honest labels; the checklist prepares material for review and does not produce a mixed demo file.

Voice Acting Vocal Texture Exercises

Explore descriptive character qualities through comfortable speech, word choice, pace, and phrasing. Compare light, steady, and playful impressions without manufacturing rasp, growls, or throat tension.

Voice Over Equipment Setup

Check the recording signal path you already own before a session. Trace microphone, interface or USB input, recording application, monitoring, and a saved test file; this is a practical setup check, not a shopping list.

Voice Acting Remote Session Checklist

Prepare the connection, local recording, script version, and handoff for a directed remote session. Keep the conversation channel separate from the recording deliverable and rehearse a connection failure before the appointment.

Voice Over Script Formatting

Prepare a readable voice-over script with stable line IDs, separate spoken copy and directions, and a visible revision. The six checks help a performer and director discuss the same line without accidentally reading production notes aloud.

Voice Acting Director Communication

Turn a direction into a specific, testable next take. Practice clarifying an ambiguous note, repeating the agreed change, and checking the result while preserving the director’s scene objective.

Audio Drama Casting Exercises

Compare roles by scene objectives and relationships rather than surface voice novelty. Use an original two-person audio scene to prepare matching audition sides and a repeatable listening rubric for a small casting rehearsal.

Voice Acting Continuity Exercises

Reconstruct a character performance after a break or out-of-order recording. Keep a private reference for relationship, pace, energy, and recording position so a later take belongs to the same scene.

Voice Over Technical Specification Checklist

Turn a supplied delivery brief into six verifiable file checks. Inspect format, sample rate, bit depth, channel count, processing, file names, and receipt in your own audio software; there is no universal client specification.

Voice Acting Portfolio Checklist

Check whether a listener can find your relevant samples, understand your role, and contact you. Organize an honest voice-acting portfolio with working media, permission notes, and one clear next action instead of an unexplained gallery.

Voice Over Pronunciation Guide

Create a small approved pronunciation sheet for names, abbreviations, numbers, and specialist terms in a recording script. Track the source and decision so a later pickup repeats the intended reading rather than an unverified guess.

Voice Over Recording Room Checklist

Compare a room’s background noise and audible reflections using a repeatable test. Check your current recording position before moving furniture or buying treatment; use saved files to distinguish outside noise from room reflections.

Live Event Voice Announcements

Prepare concise live announcements that tell an audience who needs to act, where to go, and when. Rehearse normal updates, changed information, and a correction using a fictional event instead of improvising facts over a microphone.

Voice Acting Rehearsal Schedule

Plan a bounded rehearsal around one scene objective, a baseline take, a small number of comparisons, and a review. Separate preparation, practice, and follow-up so a schedule produces observable decisions instead of an arbitrary streak.
